The core concepts, mathematical principles, implementation details, and evaluation results from the paper "CUBIC: A New TCP-Friendly High-Speed TCP Variant" (Sangtae Ha, Injong Rhee, Lisong Xu) are organized below:

1. The Problem with Standard TCP on High-BDP Networks
* Sluggish Recovery in Large BDP: Standard TCP (Reno, NewReno, SACK) increases its congestion window (cwnd) by only 1 packet per RTT (AIMD(1, 0.5)). On fast, long-distance networks with a large Bandwidth-Delay Product (BDP), recovery takes an impractical amount of time.
* The 10 Gbps Example: Over a 10 Gbps link with a 100 ms RTT and 1250-byte packets, the BDP is ~100,000 packets. If a single packet loss cuts the window to 50,000, Standard TCP takes 50,000 RTTs (~5,000 seconds or 1.4 hours) to recover full link capacity, leading to severe network underutilization.

2. Predecessor: BIC-TCP & Why CUBIC Was Created
* BIC-TCP Mechanism: BIC-TCP used a binary search between the minimum window (post-loss) and maximum window Wmax (pre-loss), followed by additive increase and "max probing".
* BIC-TCP Limitations: Its multi-phase growth function was complex to analyze and implement, and its growth remained aggressive over short RTTs.
* The CUBIC Breakthrough: CUBIC replaces the piecewise linear/logarithmic/exponential functions of BIC-TCP with a single continuous cubic function.
* Real-Time Window Growth: Crucially, CUBIC computes window growth as a function of the elapsed real time (t) since the last loss event, making growth independent of RTT. This achieves superior RTT fairness.

3. The CUBIC Mathematical Model

A. The Window Growth Function
The congestion window W(t) at elapsed time t since the last loss event is given by:
W(t) = C * (t - K)^3 + Wmax

* C: A scaling factor set to 0.4 to balance TCP-friendliness and scalability.
* Wmax: The window size right before the last congestion event.
* K: The time required to scale the window from the post-drop level back to Wmax, calculated as:
  K = ((Wmax * beta) / C)^(1/3)
  (where beta = 0.2 is the multiplicative decrease factor, meaning the window drops to Wmax * (1 - beta) = 0.8 * Wmax on packet loss).

B. The Three Operating Regions
1. TCP-Friendly Region: Standard TCP performs well under short RTTs. CUBIC calculates the theoretical window of Standard TCP as a function of real time:
   Wtcp(t) = Wmax * (1 - beta) + 3 * (beta / (2 - beta)) * (t / RTT)
   If W(t) < Wtcp(t), CUBIC matches Standard TCP's growth rate to remain strictly TCP-friendly.
2. Concave Region (t < K, cwnd < Wmax): The window accelerates quickly when far from Wmax, then flattens out to form a plateau as it approaches the previous saturation point Wmax.
3. Convex Region / Max Probing (t > K, cwnd > Wmax): If no packet loss occurs after reaching the plateau, CUBIC infers that available capacity has grown and transitions to convex growth, slowly probing for a new maximum before accelerating exponentially.

4. Key Algorithmic Enhancements
* Fast Convergence: When background flows join, existing flows must release bandwidth. If a loss occurs before reaching the prior saturation point (Wmax < Wlast_max), CUBIC reduces Wmax further:
  Wlast_max = Wmax * ((2 - beta) / 2)
  This forces the plateau to happen sooner and frees up link headroom for newer flows to catch up.
* Newton-Raphson Cubic Root Optimization: In the Linux kernel, integer arithmetic is required because floating-point operations are disabled. Transitioning from a bisection method to the Newton-Raphson approximation improved execution performance by over 10x (from 1032 clock cycles down to 79 clock cycles).
* Removal of Window Clamping: Early versions clamped the maximum window increase to Smax = 32 packets/RTT. Extensive testing confirmed that CUBIC's natural cubic curvature is stable enough to safely remove clamping in both concave and convex regions (introduced in CUBIC 2.1 / 2.2).

5. Performance & Experimental Results
* Intra-Protocol Fairness: Two competing CUBIC flows on the same bottleneck converge rapidly to a 50/50 fair share within 200 seconds across a wide range of RTTs (16 ms to 324 ms).
* Inter-RTT Fairness: Because CUBIC's window growth depends on wall-clock time rather than RTT increments, long-RTT flows are not starved by short-RTT flows. It achieves a significantly better bandwidth ratio than BIC-TCP and HSTCP.
* TCP-Friendliness: 
  * Over short RTT paths (8-10 ms), CUBIC seamlessly switches to its TCP mode and shares bandwidth evenly with TCP-SACK.
  * Over long RTT paths (82-100 ms), CUBIC scales up to claim underutilized bandwidth while leaving existing TCP traffic unharmed.
